HARD RULES (a violation makes the note unusable)
1. Source fidelity. Case Features contain only what the records state. No finding, history item, value, date or treatment that is not in the records.
2. Values. Copy every number together with its unit exactly as recorded. Do not convert units, round, or compute derived values. Keep each value attached to its time point; never merge serial results.
3. Negation. A documented negative stays negative; a documented positive stays positive.
4. Missing is not normal. If something is not documented, pending, or unavailable, say so ("not documented", "result pending"). Never describe an undocumented item as normal, negative or absent.
5. Certainty. Use "confirmed" only for diagnoses the records establish. Otherwise grade explicitly: probable / possible / cannot be excluded. Never upgrade a hypothesis into a diagnosis.
6. Done vs proposed. Interventions already carried out belong in Case Features, stated as done, only if the records say so. Everything in the plan is a proposal and must read as one ("recommend", "plan to", "consider").
7. Chronology. Preserve the recorded order and timing of events.
8. Conflicts. If records disagree, do not silently pick one version: report both, with sources, and add a review item.

CLINICAL QUALITY
- Organise the reasoning by clinical problem, most urgent first. For each problem: the supporting evidence, the evidence against (including relevant documented negatives), your graded assessment, and what is still unknown.
- Differentials must be specific to this patient: 2-4 alternatives, each with the recorded finding that raises it and the finding (or missing test) that argues against or would settle it. No generic lists. Do not list a condition the records explicitly deny as an unresolved cause; if it must be mentioned, state that the records deny it and nothing supports it.
- Do not open the discussion by copying the records' diagnosis list ("Initial diagnosis: 1. ... 2. ..."); go straight to the problem-by-problem analysis.
- Each plan item must respond to a documented problem or a stated uncertainty, and say what it is for. Cover investigations, treatment and monitoring (what, how often, and what result would change management).
- Check every proposed treatment against the documented allergies, medications, organ function, pregnancy status and anticoagulation. If one of these is undocumented and matters for the proposal, make the proposal conditional on it and add a review item. Name a drug dose only when it is standard and nothing in the records argues against it.
- Explicitly flag time-critical or life-threatening conditions suggested by the records.
- Differentials must include dangerous diagnoses suggested by the complaint and key abnormalities that are not yet excluded (e.g. pulmonary embolism with raised D-dimer and chest pain or recent immobility/surgery; hypoglycaemia with transient neurological symptoms on glucose-lowering drugs; intussusception in an infant with vomiting and abdominal pain), with the test that would exclude them. Established comorbidities are handled as co-existing problems, not as differentials.
- Case Features must include every positive exam finding (e.g. pallor, conjunctival pallor, focal tenderness) and the important negatives for the main problem. Report inconsistencies between history and exam, or between sources (e.g. two medication lists), side by side and add them to review_items.
- Before naming a specific drug or a contrast/invasive study, check age, pregnancy, allergy, liver/renal function, bleeding risk and co-medication (e.g. assess renal function before contrast with renal impairment or metformin; avoid NSAIDs until GI bleeding is excluded; do not choose a fluoroquinolone empirically while tuberculosis is not excluded; avoid anti-emetics prone to extrapyramidal effects in infants). If safety cannot be confirmed, give the drug class and the precondition only. State sedation and analgesia separately.
- When the records suggest active communicable disease (e.g. productive cough with cavitation, haemoptysis or tuberculosis contact), state isolation precautions (suspected tuberculosis: airborne isolation, single or negative-pressure room, N95); do not isolate without such symptoms or imaging. Give time frames for time-critical steps (e.g. timing of invasive assessment in high-risk acute coronary syndrome, thrombolysis/thrombectomy windows). Do not immediately repeat a test the records already answer; write "repeat if the condition changes". Inpatient monitoring frequency must match severity.
- Every diagnosis the discussion says must be excluded needs a matching test or decision point in the plan.
- The discussion must cover every problem in the records' initial diagnosis list (minor ones briefly).

LENGTH AND STYLE (an initial progress note must be quick for a senior clinician to read)
- At most 8 case-feature statements, 7 discussion statements and 9 plan items; each normally under 50 words. Choose by clinical importance; key positives, discriminating negatives and key values must stay.
- Case Features state only what the records contain: no summarising commentary, and no ratio, percentage or score that you computed yourself.
- Undocumented information: state the gaps that matter for this patient once, in a single case-feature statement, and list what must be verified under review_items. Do not repeat "conditional because X is not documented" in every plan item; where a treatment truly depends on a missing item, add the condition in a few words at the end of that item.
- Do not mention checks that do not apply to this patient (for example pregnancy status in a man).
- Report a conflict only when the difference would change diagnosis or management; different wording of the same examination is not a conflict.
- At most 6 review_items, only those that would change diagnosis or management.
